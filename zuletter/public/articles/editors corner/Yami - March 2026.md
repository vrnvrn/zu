# Building Sanctuary in Practice

*By Yami*

Over the past year, we've gotten very good at naming what's broken: surveillance, centralization, fragile systems. We are less effective at building the change we want to see. In his recent post, Vitalik reframes Ethereum's role, focusing on contributing to an ecosystem of sanctuary technologies rather than attempting to fix the world. These are tools that allow people to live, coordinate, and build with resilience under pressure.

Sanctuary technologies alone will not create the sanctuaries we hope to see. They must come together with liveable communities. Permanent Hubs provide the space where these elements unite in practice.

This is the gap.

We have end-to-end encrypted, decentralized messaging. We have crypto. We have local AI. What we lack are environments where all of this naturally forms the basic infrastructure and tools of the community, without it feeling like a burden or requiring specialist knowledge or getting in the way. 

When tools feel like an extra step rather than part of daily life, they are first to be abandoned when things get difficult. We've seen this when AWS or Cloudflare go down, when communities rely on a single internet provider, or when access to our own data depends on a subscription.

The Permanent Hubs community changes this.

We create environments where sanctuary becomes tangible and operational in practice, where coordination happens through decentralized tools, value flows through crypto native systems, and governance is tested in real conditions. We turn abstract capabilities into lived systems.
At the same time, sanctuary is not a single form. It does not resolve into one model or one unified alternative. The hubs come in all types and forms. 

At its core, sanctuary is the ability for people, families, and communities to imagine the lives they want to live and to create the conditions for those lives to emerge.

They are built on shared technological foundations, yet they express different priorities, constraints, and cultural directions. 
In ZuBerlin, experiments in co living and working, coordination, and shared infrastructure are becoming more persistent. 
ZuAfrique shows what sanctuary looks like in the real world, where decentralized tools help communities build, collaborate, and grow under real constraints.

They point toward sanctuary as something people can actually depend on in practice, shaped differently across contexts rather than converging into a single blueprint.

At the same time, hubs are evolving in their own ways. Each one is experimenting and generating valuable insights, and there is growing opportunity to connect these efforts. Sanctuary technology works like layers of a shared system, becoming most powerful when they align and work seamlessly together.

Coordination is what enables that.

It is the layer that connects isolated efforts into the ecosystem. It allows patterns to emerge, knowledge to travel, and best practices to be shared over time. It reduces repeated mistakes, surfaces early signals, and creates continuity across hubs that would otherwise remain disconnected.

Our version of sanctuary prizes the sharing of knowledge, so our own progress can't stay local. Coordination lets that knowledge be shared, so our community becomes an ecosystem.

People create spaces. Sanctuary technologies strengthen those spaces. Ethereum is one of those technologies which strengthens them by bridging the digital and real worlds. 
At its core, sanctuary is simple: 

> A physical place + a resilient digital environment = a sanctuary where people can live, build, and depend on one another. A clean digital environment for the community, one that allows it to grow, express itself, and thrive.

This is the shift ahead. Moving from tools to systems, from systems to environments, and from environments to reality.

Sanctuary technology makes it possible to build the places of the future. These are places where people do not all have to do the same thing, where distinctiveness and expression are protected, and where communities are built on consent that can only exist through freedom.

We think these places can only be built this way. Through hubs, we are beginning to show what they can look like in practice, inviting people to discover communities that reflect their values.

The invitation is open. Find your hub!
